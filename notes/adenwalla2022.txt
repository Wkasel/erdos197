Abstract
A permutation of the integers avoiding monotone arithmetic progressions of length 6 was con-
structed in (Geneson, 2018). We improve on this by construct ing a permutation of the integers
avoiding monotone arithmetic progressions of length 5. We a lso construct permutations of the
integers and the positive integers that improve on previous upper and lower density results. In
(Davis et al. 1977) they constructed a doubly inﬁnite permut ation of the positive integers that
avoids monotone arithmetic progressions of length 4. We con struct a doubly inﬁnite permuta-
tion of the integers avoiding monotone arithmetic progress ions of length 5. A permutation of the
positive integers that avoided monotone arithmetic progre ssions of length 4 with odd common dif-
ference was constructed in (LeSaulnier and Vijay, 2011). We generalise this result and show that
for each k ≥ 1, there exists a permutation of the positive integers that a voids monotone arithmetic
progressions of length 4 with common diﬀerence not divisibl e by 2 k. In addition, we specify the
structure of permutations of [1 , n] that avoid length 3 monotone arithmetic progressions mod n as
deﬁned in (Davis et al. 1977) and provide an explicit constru ction for a multiplicative result on
permutations that avoid length k monotone arithmetic progressions mod n.
1 Introduction
A permutation, a1, a 2, a 3 . . . , avoids monotone arithmetic progressions of length k if there does not
exist an increasing or decreasing subsequence of the permutation that forms a k-term arithmetic
progression. We will refer to monotone arithmetic progressions of length k as k-APs. Clearly, if a
permutation avoids k-APs then it avoids h-APs for any h > k . In [
2], Davis et al. proved that the
positive integers cannot be permuted to avoid 3-APs and can be per muted to avoid 5-APs and in [ 4],
Geneson proved that the integers can be permuted to avoid 6-APs . We prove that the integers can be
permuted to avoid 5-APs. These results leave open the questions o f whether there exist permutations
avoiding 4-APs in the integers and the positive integers. Note that if there exists a permutation, P ,
of the integers avoiding k-APs, then the subsequence of positive integers in P forms a permutation of
the positive integers avoiding k-APs. Therefore, if there does not exist a permutation of the pos itive
integers avoiding k-APs, there does not exist a permutation of the integers avoiding k-APs.

Another way of moving towards answering whether 4-AP free perm utations of the positive integers
exist is to consider permutations with restrictions on the common diﬀ erence of any 4-APs that appear.
In [ 7], it was shown that the positive integers can be permuted to avoid 4- APs with odd common
diﬀerence (all 4-APs have common diﬀerence divisible by 2). We gener alise this to show that, for any
k ≥ 1, the positive integers can be permuted to avoid 4-APs with common diﬀerence not divisible by
2k (equivalently, all 4-APs in the permutation have common diﬀerence d ivisible by 2 k).
In [ 2], it was shown that the positive integers can be arranged into a doub ly inﬁnite permutation,
. . . a −2, a −1, a 0, a 1, a 2 . . . , that avoids 4-APs but cannot be arranged in a doubly inﬁnite permut ation
that avoids 3-APs. Note that this implies that the integers cannot b e arranged in a doubly inﬁnite
permutation that avoids 3-APs. We will show that there exists a dou bly inﬁnite permutation of the
integers that avoids 5-APs. Whether there exists a doubly inﬁnite p ermutation of the integers avoiding

6 Open Questions
There are still many unknown questions in this area. Here we lay out s ome of these, including sum-
marising those we have mentioned.
Question 1 ([
2, 3, 7]). Do there exist permutations of the positive integers or the i ntegers that avoid
4-APs?
This is the most obvious open question and was asked in [ 2] in 1977, the ﬁrst paper on the topic.
Showing βZ+(4) = 1 is a step towards resolving this in the positive integer case, bu t it seems some new
techniques will need to be applied to answer it, either in showing that a ll permutations of the (positive)
integers contain 4-APs or in constructing a permutation of the (po sitive) integers that avoids them.
Question 2 ([2, 3]). Do there exists doubly inﬁnite permutations of the integers that avoid 4-APs?
It was shown in [ 2] that there exist doubly inﬁnite permutations of the positive intege rs that avoid
4-APs, but it is not known if the same is true of the integers. Note th at if there exists a permutation
of the integers, P , that avoids 4-APs then there exists a doubly inﬁnite permutation o f the integers,
2P − 1 going to the left and 2 P going to the right, that avoids 4-APs.
