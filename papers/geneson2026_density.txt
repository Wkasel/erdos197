x0 , x1 lie in one block A and x2 , x3 lie in one block B, where A and B have the same residue modulo
m and opposite signs.
Proof. No term is zero. Since the progression is nonconstant, its terms are strictly monotone as
integers. If all four have one sign, their magnitudes either shrink or grow. Lemma 5.2 or the second
part of Lemma 5.4 puts three consecutive terms in one block. Otherwise the sign changes once,
and the two maximal consecutive same-sign segments have lengths 1 and 3, 2 and 2, or 3 and 1.
Indeed, a strictly monotone integer sequence not containing zero changes sign at most once; before
the change its magnitudes shrink, and after the change they grow. In the 3 + 1 case, Lemma 5.2
puts the initial three terms in one block. In the 1 + 3 case, the first part of Lemma 5.4, with the
initial term as y, puts the terminal three terms in one block. Only the 2 + 2 case remains.
Let d be the common difference. If m | d, the shrinking pair has one block rank, so Lemma 5.1
puts it in one block. Lemma 5.3 puts the growing pair in one block. These blocks have the same
residue and opposite signs.
Suppose that m ∤ d. The shrinking pair must have increasing block ranks, since Lemma 5.1
precludes a nonincreasing block rank across a shrinking transition. The ranks are unequal because
adjacent residues differ. If the shrinking pair is negative, then ρ(x0 ) < ρ(x1 ). Equation (2.3) gives
ρ(x2 ) < ρ(x3 ), strictly, so q(x2 ) > q(x3 ) because positive blocks use the reverse residue order. If
the shrinking pair is positive, then ρ(x0 ) > ρ(x1 ), so (2.3) gives ρ(x2 ) > ρ(x3 ), strictly. Hence
q(x2 ) > q(x3 ) for the negative growing pair. In either case, Lemma 5.3 would put the growing pair
in one block although its residues are different. Thus m ∤ d cannot occur when two terms have each
sign.
